# Strict Video Production Brief

## Ownership rule

The final hackathon video is produced separately by Codex after the project is complete and verified. The coding agent must prepare the deterministic demo state, final application, real screenshots, demo script, storyboard inputs, and recording-safe build, but must **not** synthesize, render, upload, publish, or substitute the final video.

The final video workflow must follow the template below strictly. Project-specific values are filled only from verified project evidence. No UI, result, number, quotation, interaction, or deployment state may be invented.

---

# TASK: Create a [LENGTH, e.g. 2:30] [TYPE: product demo / explainer / promo / tutorial] video

## 1. Goal and audience
- Purpose: [what the video must achieve, e.g. "win over hackathon judges"]
- Audience: [who watches, and what they already know]
- The one thing viewers must remember: [one sentence]

## 2. Source material
- Use my real footage and screenshots as the main visuals: [list of files/screens].
- Do not invent UI, numbers, quotes, or results. If something is missing, tell me exactly what to record or provide instead.

## 3. Format
- Aspect ratio: [16:9 / 9:16 / 1:1], resolution [1920x1080], [30] fps
- Length: [target], hard max [limit]
- Export: MP4 (H.264) plus .srt captions. Optional extras: [vertical cut, thumbnail]

## 4. Style
- Tone: [calm / energetic / professional / friendly]
- Palette: [hex codes] and font: [font name]
- Look: [flat and clean / cinematic / editorial]. Avoid: [neon, gradients, stock clip-art, glitch effects]
- Motion: gentle zooms, soft fades, highlight pulses on what is being discussed. No excessive transitions.

## 5. Structure (scene plan)
| Time | Scene | Visual | Narration goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | Hook | [...] | Grab attention with the problem |
| 0:15-0:35 | Problem | [...] | Who suffers and why |
| 0:35-1:50 | Solution / demo | [...] | Show it working, slowly |
| 1:50-2:15 | Key features | [...] | One line each |
| 2:15-2:30 | Close | [...] | Tagline + call to action |

## 6. Voiceover
- Voice: [gender, accent, pace ~140 wpm, warm/neutral], natural pauses between scenes.
- Script: [paste your script, or write "write the script yourself from the scene plan, max N words, and show it to me before rendering"].
- Keep any real recorded audio from my footage untouched (only normalize volume).

## 7. Audio
- Royalty-free background music, [mood], about -22 dB under speech, ducked during narration.
- Subtle UI sounds only when something appears on screen.

## 8. Captions and accessibility
- Burn-in captions in [font], high contrast, inside safe margins.
- Subtitle any non-English speech with translations.

## 9. Editing rules
- Cut dead time and loading waits over 1 second.
- Blur or hide private data (keys, emails, personal numbers, unrelated tabs).
- Smooth cursor, soft ring on clicks.
- Show real data and timestamps only.

## 10. Deliverables
1. Final video + captions file
2. [Optional] short vertical cut, thumbnail
3. A list of anything missing that I still need to provide
4. Show me the script and storyboard first, and wait for approval before rendering

## ClarityRail binding

When this template is used for ClarityRail:

- The final length and hard maximum must follow the verified Taskforge submission rules and the finished `docs/DEMO_SCRIPT.md`.
- The main story must prove the intended user, the overwhelming brief, the four-part Clarity Contract, and the meaningful before/after change.
- The demonstration must use the real final deployment or a clearly identified local build of the final commit.
- The video must show actual Evidence Thread, Ambiguity Gate, Change Impact Guard, Dependency-Safe Detour, offline resume, and handoff behavior only if each is working and verified.
- Use ClarityRail's final repository palette, locally bundled fonts, thumbnail system, architecture assets, and real application captures.
- Voice delivery must sound natural, clear, confident, and human-paced. Do not use robotic cadence, exaggerated advertising language, or unsupported winning claims.
- The first script and storyboard are review artifacts. Rendering begins only after the user approves them.
